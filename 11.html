before the Islamic

Palestine has been Arab since the Canaanites settled there.

Life in the region that later became known as Palestine is not known for certain. However, the oldest archaeological discoveries, found in Jabal al-Qafrah south of Nazareth and the sandy slopes near Tiberias, dating back to between 7500 and 3100 BC, confirm that the region witnessed a simple way of life. The most important event was the founding of the city of Jericho, which historians consider the oldest town in history. Remains of this city have been found near the town of Ain al-Sultan.

Canaanite migration

The first significant human migrations to Palestine began at the beginning of the third millennium BC, and were the migrations of the Canaanites, who were known by the names of the places where they settled. After a period, there were three languages: Canaanite, Aramaic - the language of Christ, peace be upon him - and Arabic. Palestine remained called the land of Canaan until 1200 BC, when it was invaded by Cretan tribes.

The migration of Abraham, peace be upon him

In the third millennium BC, Abraham, peace be upon him, migrated from the town of Ur in Iraq to Palestine, and there he gave birth to Isaac, the father of Jacob, who is also called Israel, and to whom the Israelites are descended.

Egyptian Empire

During that period, Palestine was part of the Egyptian Empire, and trade exchanges were active between them, as indicated by the Tell el-Amarna letters discovered in Upper Egypt.

The name of Palestine

Palestine witnessed a series of invasions by Cretan tribes who settled on the shores of Jaffa and Gaza. The region was named Palestine after the name of the invading Cretan tribe that merged with the Canaanites, the original inhabitants of the country. The name Palestine was given to all the coastal and inland lands inhabited by the Canaanites. Over time, the Canaanite element prevailed and the entire population of the country became Canaanite Arabs.

Israelis

Due to the famine that swept Palestine, Jacob (peace be upon him) and his sons migrated to Egypt, where his son, the Prophet Joseph (peace be upon him), was in charge of its treasuries. This story is detailed in the Holy Quran in Surat Yusuf. The Israelites settled in Egypt and their numbers increased, but they began to face persecution during the reign of Ramses II. Moses (peace be upon him) decided to take them to the land of Canaan. This story is mentioned in the Holy Quran in numerous places.

Dawod

Dawod, peace be upon him, assumed the throne and succeeded in uniting the Israelites once again, resolving the disputes and wars that had existed between them. He defeated the Jebusites and established the Kingdom of Israel, making Jerusalem the capital of his kingdom. After the death of Solomon, son of David, peace be upon them, in 935 BC, the kingdom was divided against itself. Judah rose in Jerusalem, and Israel in Samaria. Disputes and wars broke out between the two kingdoms, and each sought the help of the kings of Egypt or Assyria against the other. This weakened both of them and weakened their authority over the population, leading to unrest once again.

The demise of the kingdoms of Judah and Israel

In 920 BC, King Shoshenq of Egypt attacked and occupied the Kingdom of Judah, which subsequently became a vassal state of Egypt. In 721 BC, the Assyrians attacked and occupied the Kingdoms of Israel and Judah, imposing tribute on them. The Kingdom of Israel attempted a rebellion, but the Assyrians violently suppressed the rebellion and took most of its population captive to Iraq.

In 597 BC, the Chaldean king Nebuchadnezzar launched an attack on Palestine, capturing Jerusalem, the capital of Judah, and taking its king, his family, and most of its leaders captive to Iraq. He established a new king in Jerusalem. In 586 BC, the remnants of the Jews attempted to rebel against the Babylonian sultan in Palestine. Nebuchadnezzar returned and invaded again. This time, he destroyed Jerusalem, and Palestine returned to being a Canaanite Arab state, subordinate to Iraq, receiving Arab migrants from Syria and the Arabian Peninsula.

Palestine has been a target for invaders since ancient times.
